## 7) Why These Problems Are Hard

1. Source quality mismatch:
- PLEAC Perl files often contain explanatory fragments, not executable scripts.

2. Runtime assumptions:
- many recipes assume files, devices, daemons, network, or privileged operations.

3. Converter scope limits:
- auto converters work best on deterministic, runnable scripts with stable stdout.

4. Semantic gaps:
- some Perl idioms rely on context/reflection/dynamic behavior not directly represented in SLUP runtime.

## 8) Recommended Resolution Plan

Phase A (largest immediate yield):
1. Convert remaining **non-standalone-perl-snippet (24)** by manual semantic SLUP ports.
2. Convert **manual-port-required (13)** similarly.
3. Skip sections that fundamentally require reference/coderef semantics.

Phase B (environment/dependency):
1. For missing-files/path sections (8), add deterministic fixtures or simplified in-repo inputs.
2. Skip missing-module sections (8) as out-of-scope.

Phase C (singletons):
1. Timeout section: add bounded input/stub behavior.
2. Newline mismatch: decide whether strict byte parity is required.
3. Encoding/control chars: add binary-safe converter path.
4. Permission/network case: mock side effects or keep deterministic textual demo.
